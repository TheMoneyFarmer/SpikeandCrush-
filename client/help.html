How to Play

The short version, if you read nothing else on this page.

Spike & Crush is a competitive trading game. Every match, you and a group of opponents start with $10,000 in virtual capital and a fixed amount of time. You buy and sell real historical price data on live-feeling charts, trying to end the match with the highest profit. Everyone also gets sabotage cards - small, one-time abilities you can use against opponents to disrupt their trading at the worst possible moment.

The Basic Loop

  1. Pick a game mode and enter a match (most modes cost coins to enter).
  2. Trade the instruments in that match - buy when you expect price to rise, sell when you expect it to fall.
  3. Use your sabotage cards against opponents when the timing is right.
  4. When the timer hits zero, whoever has the highest profit (P&L) wins the largest share of the prize pool.
  5. Ranked matches also adjust your War Rating based on how you finished.

You Never Risk Real Money in a Match

The $10,000 starting capital is entirely virtual - it exists only for that one match and resets every time. The only real stake is your coin entry fee, which you can win back (and then some) by playing well.

Trading Basics

For players who have never traded anything before.

What Is a Pip?

A pip is the smallest standard unit of price movement for an instrument. For a pair like EURUSD, a move from 1.0850 to 1.0851 is one pip. Different instruments use different pip sizes - gold (XAUUSD) and indices move in larger increments than currency pairs.

What Is a Lot Size?

Lot size controls how large your position is, and therefore how much each pip of movement is worth to you in dollars. A bigger lot size means bigger swings in your P&L for the same price movement - both up and down.

What Is a Spread?

The spread is the gap between the buy (ask) price and the sell (bid) price. It's the built-in cost of opening a trade - you pay it once, immediately, the moment you enter a position. Tighter spreads are cheaper to trade.

What Is Stop Loss / Take Profit?

Stop Loss (SL) automatically closes your trade once price moves a set amount against you, capping your downside. Take Profit (TP) automatically closes your trade once price reaches a target in your favour, locking in a win before it can reverse. Both can be set in pips or at an exact price, and dragged directly on the chart mid-trade.

What Is Leverage?

Every player trades at 100:1 leverage, so a position only ties up 1% of its full value as margin. There is no lot limit: how big you can go depends on your free margin. On EUR/USD near 1.08, one lot needs about $1,080 of margin, so $10,000 opens about 9 lots.

What Is Margin?

Margin used is what your open positions tie up. Free margin is your equity minus margin used, and it decides what you can still open. Margin level is equity divided by margin used, as a percentage.

Margin Call and Stop Out

At a 100% margin level you get a margin call: a warning, and no new trades until it recovers. At 50% you are stopped out: your biggest losing position is closed, then the next, until the level recovers. You keep trading with what is left. You can hold up to 10 positions at once.

The 2-Minute Lock

One more brake: lose 20% of your starting capital within a 2-minute window and new trades pause until the next window starts. In matches only.

Quick War

The full game. Best for your everyday match. You start with 5 cards (new players with only 4 unlocked cards start with 4). Win trades in a row to earn more cards, wear the crown when you lead, chase quick missions and trade the spike alerts. Ranked: it moves your War Rating. Start here if you are new.

Blitz War

A three-minute sprint on the busiest markets. Best for a quick fix. You start with 5 cards and they refill fast: one win earns a card, two in a row earn two. No heavy (epic or legendary) cards. The instruments come from the most active markets in the catalog and spike alerts come more often. The result screen shows your fastest winning trade.

Grand War

Twenty minutes of strategy. Best for when you have time to plan. You start with 5 cards and can change them in the lobby. Three market events are scheduled near minutes 5, 10 and 15: each is one of the biggest real moves of that session, taken from the price data, never invented. The direction is never shown in advance. At minute 10 the half-time card shows the top five. The Steady Hand badge goes to whoever in the top three had the smallest dip from their peak.

Elimination

Survive the cuts. Best for big lobbies and nerves of steel. Every 3 minutes (at 3, 6, 9 and 12 minutes) the bottom 20% of the players still in are knocked out, at least one each time. The match strip shows Cut in 0:30 before each cut, and players in the bottom group carry a Danger tag. When you are cut, your open positions are closed at that moment and you can keep watching. Your final place depends on when you went out: players who survive longer always finish above those cut earlier. Cuts need at least 4 players at the start and stop when fewer than 4 remain. The Lifeline card (unlocks at level 6) protects you from the next cut, once per match. User-made bracket tournaments are separate: see Tournaments.

Solo Ranked

Free and always open. Best for learning and beating your personal best. You trade against 3 AI traders with their own personalities, so there is never a wait. You get 5 cards like every mode, and you can use them on the AI traders. The match strip shows the ghost of your own best Solo run, second by second, and your best result from yesterday to beat. Missions come as a chain of three: finish one to unlock the next. Ranked: it moves your separate Solo rating.

Daily Challenge

One market for everyone today. Best for one go a day and bragging rights. Every player who enters on the same day trades the exact same historical window, with no live opponents and no cards. The strip shows the ghost of today's best run so far. After your run you can send a challenge link: your friend plays the exact same market and sees your run as their ghost.

Private War

Your room, your rules. Best for playing with friends. Create a room, share the 6 digit code and choose the house rules: cards on or off, deck level (basic, basic and mid, or full), starting hand, Elimination cuts on or off, and the card style (Quick War or Blitz). Everyone sees the rules in the room before the start. Hosting costs 1 coin, charged only when the match starts; guests play free.

Glossary

Equity
Your balance plus the open profit or loss on your positions. The leaderboard and the winner use equity.
P&L
Profit and loss: your equity minus the $10,000 you started with.
Card slot
One place in your hand for a card. Quick War has 4, Blitz 2.
Streak
Winning trades in a row. Streaks earn cards.
Crown
The current leader. Some cards can only target the crown.
Spike alert
A warning that a big real move is coming from the price data in the next seconds. The direction is never shown.
Market event
Grand War only: one of the biggest real moves of the session, scheduled near minutes 5, 10 and 15.
Cut
Elimination only: the moment the bottom 20% are knocked out.
Danger
Elimination only: you are in the group that would be cut if the cut happened now.
Lifeline
A defense card for Elimination that protects you from the next cut, once per match.
Ghost
A previous run you race against: your best Solo run, today's best Daily Challenge run, or the run of the friend who challenged you.
Heat
A separate score for playing aggressively. It never changes P&L, rank, prizes or ratings.
Steady Hand
Grand War badge for the top-three finisher with the smallest dip from their peak.

Sabotage Cards

30 attack cards and 3 defensive cards (Shield, Cleanse, and Lifeline for Elimination). Every mode with cards deals 5 at the start and you hold up to 5; each can be played once. Every attack card changes something real for its target. A player hit by a card is protected for 10 seconds, and no single card can take anyone below half the starting capital.

Streaks, Heat, Missions and Spikes

Earning cards

Win trades in a row to earn cards. A win counts when the trade closes in profit of at least 0.2% of your starting capital and was held at least 5 seconds. Streak 1 and 2 give one card each, streak 3 gives two, streak 5 refills every empty slot, and each win after that gives one more. A losing close resets the streak; you keep your cards. With a full hand, the reward becomes Heat.

Heat

Heat is a bonus score shown on the result screen. Your streak multiplies it (x1, x1.5, x2, x3). It never changes your P&L, rank, prizes or rating.

The crown

The match leader wears the crown. Overtake them and you earn one card (at most once a minute). Hitting the leader with a card earns Heat.

Quick missions

Every match shows two small missions above the chart. Each one finished pays a few coins, with a cap per match and per day. Tiny trades don't count.

Spike alerts

Before a real big move in the market data, the strip above the chart shows a countdown. It never says the direction or size, and it never covers the chart. Only real moves from the data are announced.

Market speed

Matches replay the busiest stretches of real market data. If a mode ever plays faster than real time, a MARKET badge next to the clock shows the multiplier. Right now every mode plays at 1x.

Levels and unlocks

New players start with four starter cards. Playing earns XP; levels and wins unlock the rest (see the Rank page and the Card collection). Unlocks are earned by playing only. Coins never buy a card.

Ghost challenges

Challenge a friend to the exact market you just played. They see your result to beat. Challenges are free, unranked, and expire after 7 days.

Scoring & Ratings

How War Rating Works

Ranked matches adjust your War Rating based on where you finish: 1st place gains 25 rating, 2nd gains 10, 3rd is unchanged, every other position loses 10, and finishing dead last loses 25. There's also a small additional adjustment (±5) if the opponents you played were notably stronger or weaker rated than you.

Prize Pool Distribution

Every player's entry coins go into the match's prize pool. The platform keeps 15%, and the remaining 85% is split by finishing position: 1st place gets 50% of it, 2nd gets 25%, 3rd gets 15%, and 4th gets 10%. Lower finishes in larger matches (Grand War) don't receive a prize share.

Draw Rules

If two or more players finish with the exact same P&L (to the cent), they share every rank position their group collectively occupies - their combined prize percentage is split evenly between the players in the tie, and their rating change is averaged across those positions. Solo Ranked never registers a draw at all, since it's always exactly one human against AI.

Battle Pass

A monthly progression track alongside normal matches. Everyone has a Free Track that earns coins and basic rewards from playing; a parallel Premium Track (not on sale right now) has exclusive cosmetics and bigger coin rewards at every tier.

Earning XP

Every match gives XP, winning gives a bonus on top, and completing daily/weekly challenges gives more. Progress through tiers by playing - there's no way to buy tiers directly.

Challenges

Daily challenges refresh every day and are quick, achievable goals. Weekly challenges refresh every week, are harder, and reward more XP and coins for consistent, skilled play across the week.

Season Reset

Each season lasts a fixed number of days. When it ends, tier progress resets for the new season's track, but every reward you already claimed stays in your inventory permanently.

Coins

How Coins Work

Coins are the platform's in-game currency. New accounts start with a welcome bonus. You earn more by winning matches, finishing challenges, and battle pass tiers. You spend them on match entry fees and cosmetic shop items.

Buying Coins

Coins are not on sale right now. To earn more, invite friends: you get 100 coins for each friend who finishes their first match against other real players, up to 50 paid invites. Get your link on the Wallet page.

Match Entry Fees

Entry fees vary by mode (see the Game Modes table above) and are deducted the moment you join a match, going straight into that match's prize pool.

Prizes

Prize coins are credited to your balance automatically the instant a match ends - no manual claim step. Tournament War and competition prizes work the same way (credited the moment the bracket or competition concludes).

Withdrawals

Coins are a closed-loop in-game balance. Coins have no cash value and cannot be withdrawn or exchanged for money - including coins won in matches, tournaments and competitions.

Account
